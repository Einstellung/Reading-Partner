**1. CONTRADICTIONS.**

The sharpest conflict is about Live2D on iOS, and the digest's own fact-check is the party that is wrong. Dimension 1 claimed the Cubism Web SDK supports Safari on iOS; its fact-check "refuted" this, reporting that Live2D's compatibility table marks Safari for macOS only. I fetched the primary source (https://www.live2d.com/en/sdk/about/): the Safari row reads Windows – / macOS ○ / Linux – / Android – / **iOS ○**. Dimensions 2, 3 and 5 all assumed iOS support and were right. The correction should also have died on inspection: every browser on iOS is WebKit, so "Chrome on iOS supported, Safari on iOS not" cannot describe a real engine capability. What survives is a narrower caveat nobody stated — Live2D lists *Safari*, and a Tauri app is WKWebView under a `tauri://` custom scheme, which is untested by anyone.

Second, the three dimensions disagree about which Rive package to buy, and Dimension 1's headline number is the wrong one. Dimension 1 sells "222 KB brotli for canvas-lite" as the adoption cost; Dimensions 2 and 3 both establish that Vector Feathering — the feature that renders the concept art's glow as vector soft-light rather than a texture fake — exists only in the WebGL2 runtime. Re-confirmed at https://rive.app/docs/runtimes/web/canvas-vs-webgl: webgl2 "✅ Supported", canvas "❌ Not yet supported; planned for a future release". So the real figure is `@rive-app/webgl2` at 2,004,858 B of wasm plus 413,126 B of JS, next to the 4,633,788 B pdfium.wasm the app already ships — +43% wasm weight, not a category change, but ten times what Dimension 1 quoted. Two further corrections neither dimension made: Rive's own size page (https://rive.app/docs/runtimes/runtime-sizes, last updated January 2026) says those figures cover the **wasm only**, excluding the ~93 KB gzipped JS; and brotli numbers are irrelevant to a Tauri app, which fetches nothing over a network — you pay bundle bytes and wasm instantiation, so the honest number is ~2.4 MB on disk. Dimension 1's counter-argument (Rive's note that canvas is faster for blend modes on mobile) is reconcilable rather than contradictory: avoid non-Normal blend modes and the WebGL2 penalty does not arise.

Third, and most consequential: **Dimension 3's entire lipsync plan is architecturally incompatible with Dimension 5's audio findings, and with a decision this repo already made.** Dimension 3's cheap path is wawa-lipsync running an `AnalyserNode` over audio playing in the page; its upgrade path is a Cartesia/Azure WebSocket opened from the page. Dimension 5 establishes (WebKit 167788) that WKWebView ignores the host `AVAudioSession`, so it is all-native or all-webview. The repo already chose all-native — `docs/33-语音简报.md:23`: 「音频链路整体在 Swift：麦克风采集、回声消除、SpeechAnalyzer 识别、TTS 播放。TTS 的网络请求放 Rust 侧，音频字节直接进 Swift 播放，不经过 webview」. If no audio ever reaches the page, there is no buffer to FFT and wawa-lipsync has nothing to analyse; and the CSP change Dimension 3 recommends (`connect-src`) is unnecessary because the vendor socket lives in Rust. Track 1 is costed against an architecture the project rejected on 2026-08-09.

Fourth, Dimension 4 contradicts the repo wholesale, not just Dimension 5. Its "realistic first build is a Tauri Swift plugin holding AVAudioEngine + VPIO + SpeechTranscriber" is `plugins/voice`, shipped and measured over 28 real holds. Its TTS survey never mentions SiliconFlow CosyVoice2-0.5B, which `docs/33` selected months ago. Its verdict that `AVSpeechSynthesizer` is a dead end because it cannot stream conflicts with `docs/33`, which uses it as the offline fallback *and* reads `willSpeakRangeOfSpeechString` for character-level timing, and with Dimension 3's finding that Apple emits `AVSpeechSynthesisMarker.Mark.phoneme`. Sentence-by-sentence enqueueing is what every cloud TTS chunker does anyway; "cannot stream" overstates it into a false dead end. Dimensions 4 and 5 also flatly invert on getUserMedia ([ios-no] vs [ios-yes]); both are right about different halves — it works foregrounded, it is muted on backgrounding — and it is moot under the native architecture.

Two numeric conflicts worth recording. Dimension 6's Character.AI figures are internally inconsistent: $16.7M ÷ 233M MAU = $0.072 ARPU, not the $0.72 quoted, which implies 23.3M MAU. That is the same 万-conversion error class the fact-checker already caught in the same 人人都是产品经理 article (96万 read as 96K); both numbers from that source should be discounted. And on blink rate, Dimension 6 said 15–20/min, its fact-check "refuted" with 20–50/min, and neither has the number that matters: ~17/min in a lab setting, dropping to **4–5/min while reading** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blinking). The user's own eye is locked while reading, which is the physiological corroboration of the −28% comprehension finding, and neither dimension surfaced it. Finally, the Opus 5 thinking correction is right but incomplete: Opus 5 accepts `{type:"disabled"}` only at effort ≤ high, and Anthropic's current guidance warns that disabling thinking on Opus 5 makes it write tool calls into visible text and leak `<thinking>` tags — fatal in a voice loop that calls page-reading tools. The voice-turn model is `claude-haiku-4-5` ($1/$5 per MTok, **200K context**, not 1M — a real constraint against this app's book-sized context) or `claude-sonnet-5` at low effort, never a de-thought Opus 5.

**2. COVERAGE GAPS.**

The biggest one falls exactly between Dimension 3 (lipsync) and Dimension 5 (platform): **when the audio is native and the character is in the WebView, where does mouth data come from and at what rate?** No dimension asked. There are three answers and the good one is already half-built. The existing plugin emits `{kind:"level"}` at ~10 Hz — fine for a glow or body pulse, too coarse for a mouth against ITU's 45 ms-lead / 125 ms-lag budget. Streaming levels at 30–60 Hz is the obvious fix and the wrong one: on iOS the Swift→Rust channel is a JSON C-string (`*const c_char` parsed as `serde_json::Value`), so every frame is a separate `webview.eval` on the main thread beside a 60 fps render. The right shape is a **per-sentence contract**: Swift already knows the sentence text and `AVAudioPlayerNode.playerTime`, and `docs/33` already linearly interpolates character position from "this sentence is N chars over duration T" for barge-in truncation. Send `{sentenceId, text, startHostTime, durationMs}` once per sentence (~one message every two seconds), and let the WebView drive the mouth off its own rAF clock, resyncing at each boundary. A stylized blob needs an open-amount envelope, not 15 visemes; bilabial closure — the failure everyone worries about — does not exist on a face with no lips. And for the offline path it is free: `AVSpeechSynthesizer.write(_:toBufferCallback:toMarkerCallback:)` hands you buffers and markers together, so audio and phoneme timings arrive before playback. Confirmed in passing: `setVoiceProcessingEnabled`/`isVoiceProcessingEnabled` are declared on the shared `AVAudioIONode` superclass (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudioionode), i.e. one VPIO unit serves both directions — which is why routing TTS through the engine's own output is what makes the AEC reference signal correct, exactly as `docs/33:27` says.

The second gap is geographic. Dimensions 1 and 6 covered California SB 243, Apple 5.1.2(i) and the 13+/16+/18+ questionnaire. The developer is Chinese, the UI copy will be Chinese, and nobody mentioned Chinese law. Two instruments bite a talking companion. 《生成式人工智能服务管理暂行办法》 (https://www.cac.gov.cn/2023-07/13/c_1690898327029107.htm): Article 2 scopes it to services provided 向中华人民共和国境内公众; Article 17 requires 算法备案 for services with 舆论属性或者社会动员能力; Article 10 requires providers to 采取有效措施防范未成年人用户过度依赖或者沉迷 — a character engineered for attachment is the paradigm case of that clause, and it is the Chinese analogue of SB 243's minor duties. More immediately binding on design: 《人工智能生成合成内容标识办法》, in force **2025-09-01** (https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-03/14/c_1743654684782215.htm), Article 4 requires 显式标识 — a prominent notice in the interactive interface, and for AI-generated **audio** specifically 语音提示或者音频节奏提示 or a prominent label in the interface. That is a constraint on how the character is allowed to speak, not paperwork. The scope carve-out matters and cuts the right way for now: a TestFlight build for one person is not 向境内公众提供服务; the China App Store is.

Third, `docs/33` names exactly one blocking unknown — SiliconFlow's per-sentence TTFB, "按句接力成不成立的唯一未知数" — and nobody tried to shrink it. Half of it is settleable from the docs: the endpoint takes `stream` defaulting to **true** and emits `pcm` at 8000/16000/24000/32000/44100 Hz (https://docs.siliconflow.com/cn/api-reference/audio/create-speech), so 16 kHz PCM feeds `AVAudioPlayerNode` at the VPIO's own rate with no resample and no decode step. What is left is network TTFB from the device, which is a twenty-minute measurement, not a research question. The cost consequence is the number Dimension 4 should have led with: ¥0.05 per 1000 UTF-8 bytes, three bytes per Chinese character, ≈¥0.00015/char; at ~250 spoken chars/min that is ¥0.0375/min of speech, so a companion that speaks 30 s of each minute costs about **¥1.1/hour (≈$0.15)**, with ASR free on-device and a Haiku-class turn adding roughly $0.10–0.20/hour. That is cheaper than every one of Dimension 4's five costed stacks, using a vendor it never mentions.

A fourth, smaller gap: nobody asked where the character physically goes on an iPad reading page. The repo's own pitfalls settle it — pitfall 143 (the system selection callout is a 44 px UIKit bar, invisible to `elementFromPoint`, that swallows touches) and pitfall 86 (promoting a layer drops LCD subpixel antialiasing) — and Dimension 6's comprehension evidence says the character must be still while the eye is on the page. Together they leave one safe placement: outside the text column, in the chat rail on iPad landscape, animating only in the gaps after a page turn or a highlight.

**3. THE LOAD-BEARING UNKNOWNS.**

*Does `SpeechTranscriber.isAvailable` return true on the target iPad?* `docs/33` lists this as untested; community reports cover iPhone and Mac only. If it is false, free on-device Mandarin ASR vanishes and the hourly cost roughly triples. Test: one line added to the existing parameter-driven probe, one TestFlight build, one minute of use.

*Does a Rive WebGL2 character hold 60 fps beside the PDFium viewport in the real WKWebView, and what does it cost in battery and heat?* No published benchmark exists for this combination and it is the number that decides the runtime. Test in a day: author a three-state `.riv`, load it with `RuntimeLoader.setWasmBinary()` (an in-memory ArrayBuffer, per https://rive.app/docs/runtimes/web/rive-parameters — this sidesteps the CSP and COEP fetch question entirely, which no dimension noticed), run a scripted 20-minute read on the Mac mini build loop with Instruments Energy Log, and diff against the same session with the character hidden. Related and cheaper: Dimension 2's claim that rAF is "hard-capped at 60 Hz in WKWebView by Apple's design" is not supported by its own source — WebKit bug 173434 shows 120 Hz rAF shipping on iOS 18 and comment #77 (2025-11-05) confirming 120 fps on an iPad Pro M5; what actually caps you is the "Prefer Page Rendering Updates near 60fps" flag defaulting on. Budget 60 fps anyway, but stop treating it as immutable.

*Does VPIO + SpeechAnalyzer survive open-ended listening rather than hold-to-talk?* Everything measured in the repo is a hold with a five-minute backstop; a companion you can talk to at any moment is a 45-minute open microphone with the orange dot lit throughout. Test: raise the backstop, run 45 minutes, log final/volatile continuity, watch for recognizer stalls, and read the battery delta.

*Does the per-sentence mouth contract land inside 45 ms lead / 125 ms lag across the IPC?* Test: play a fixed sentence, screen-record at 60 fps with audio, count frames between mouth-open and first waveform energy. Half a day on the existing iPad debug channel.

*Can a non-artist produce a `.riv` that reads as alive?* This is the largest schedule risk and the cheapest to test: buy one month of Cadet ($17; "No runtime fee. Your exports keep working forever… Rive files don't phone home or depend on an active subscription", https://rive.app/blog/rive-s-new-9-mo-plan), spend a day in the browser editor — which runs on Linux, unlike Cubism — and produce the blob with blink, breath and three states. If a day does not get there, commission it or cut the feature; do not discover this in month three.

**4. THE HONEST VERDICT.**

The brief handed to the researchers was wrong in a way that miscalibrated half the digest. This app does not have zero audio: `plugins/voice` is a shipping Swift Tauri plugin with `.playAndRecord`/`.voiceChat`, hardware AEC, on-device `SpeechAnalyzer`, and 28 holds of measured latency (1082 ms cold / 304 ms warm, the ~690 ms being `setVoiceProcessingEnabled` rebuilding the VPIO unit); `src/ai/voice` has a desktop record→STT→polish path; `docs/27` and `docs/33` already fix the cascade, barge-in truncation, VAD choice, TTS vendor, sentence-splitting rules, App Review framing ("按后台播放的语音助手报，不按后台录音报"), and a measured full-duplex result (VPIO on, speaker at full volume, recognizer emits nothing for 15 s, user interjection recognized instantly). And `docs/north-star/companion.md` already specifies this exact feature and lists what is missing. The audio half is roughly 60% built and 100% specified. The character half is 0% of both. **Treat this as an art-and-behaviour project with an audio dependency, not a voice project.**

What is genuinely achievable, in this order. First, ship the character with **no voice at all**: a Rive WebGL2 state machine, authored to the concept sheet's eight emotions and five idles, driven from events the app already emits — page turn, highlight, tool-call start, observation distilled, token budget consumed. Blink, breath and gaze-follow are three sine-and-random loops with published constants, they belong in a `.ts` module under a new layer entry in `tests/layering.test.ts`, and they need no SDK and no licence. That is a two-week feature that delivers most of "alive" and can be judged before a microphone is involved. Second, build the native output path — `docs/33`'s M-voice-2: Rust fetches PCM, Swift plays it through the same engine so the AEC reference is correct. The daily info briefing already justifies this work; the companion inherits it. Third, bind the mouth and the emotion states to the per-sentence contract. Fourth, M-voice-3: VAD, turn detection, barge-in truncation. Fifth, and only after weeks of living with it, proactive speech — in-session only, driven by observations, with a strict floor on frequency.

The traps, named. Any path that leaves the WebView: Tauri cannot make the iOS WebView transparent (tauri#10152, open since 2024-06-29 with no PR), so a native UIView over the WKWebView is bespoke work you would own forever, and Unity retains 110 MB unloaded. Live2D specifically: legally sound at this revenue scale, but the Core and Framework cannot be committed to a PolyForm-NC public repo, the editor has no Linux build, and its whole value is rigging occluded anime features that a radially symmetric blob does not have. An energy bar that drains while the user is away. A fully human TTS voice on a non-human sprite — that is the measured mismatch condition, and it is also the more expensive one. Animating anything while the user's eyes are on the page. Proactive speech from outside the app, which needs a server the project does not have and is capped at three background pushes an hour with 30 s of runtime. And de-thinking Opus 5 to make it fast.

The strongest case against building it at all is strong and should be stated plainly. Every retention datapoint in this survey points the same way: Chinese companion apps lost 77–81% of downloads in five months of 2025 with D3 retention under 20% and 5–7 days of engagement per character; xAI retired the highest-profile animated companion after twelve months; Replika, the realism incumbent, watched ARR fall from $14M to $4.8M. The one eye-tracked test of animation beside narrative content found a 28% comprehension drop from irrelevant motion and **no benefit** from relevant motion; the pedagogical-agent meta-analysis puts the whole agent effect at g = −0.053 on cognitive load. This app's actual differentiator is that the AI has read the book — a property a face does not improve and actively competes with for pixels on the single device the app targets. The character also imports three regulatory regimes (SB 243's companion-chatbot duties, Apple's AI age-rating and 5.1.2(i) consent, China's 备案 plus mandatory AI-audio labelling) that a reading tool with a text panel does not carry. If "close to a real person" is the goal, the cheapest 90% of it is a 200 ms reaction and a memory of what you said two days ago — LOVOT buys attachment with 0.2–0.4 s latency, 37 °C and no language at all — and neither needs a sprite.

The rebuttal that survives: this is a single-user tool built by its own user, and every one of those statistics measures the acquisition and retention of strangers, not whether the owner of the instrument enjoys playing it. `docs/north-star/companion.md` already committed to the form. So the conclusion is not "don't build it" — it is: build the character first and silently, keep it off the page while the eyes are on the page, let the voice arrive later on the rails `docs/33` already laid, and hold the whole thing to the one question the evidence does support, which is whether it makes the user open the app tomorrow.
